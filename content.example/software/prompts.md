Prompt for Resume:
Suggest line by line edits for my \[RESUME\] for this role at \[COMPANY\]. Identify the 5 most pressing problems the company is solving and has mentioned in the JD and then include them in the Resume. Be concise, and focus on my strengths. Make the resume ATS friendly and make sure to include keywords for high impact. Under each work experience, put the most relevant tools/skills that were used for the role. Make sure the professional summary is concise and not more than 50 words.
Use the Google XYZ technique to write bullet points: "Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y], by doing [Z]" to clearly demonstrate impact. Use numbers to quantify descriptions wherever possible rather than vague terms like "several" or "many". Use action verbs. Refer to the sample resume template provided in the text below and frame the bullet points accordingly.

This is a software engineering application: every bullet should read as engineering work, naming the stack, the design decision and the measurable result. Keep the PROJECTS section full.

Prompt for Cover letter:
Write a professional cover letter for this role using my updated resume that you have edited and the JD. Have three paragraphs: one is introduction, second is role and company fit, third is a concluding paragraph explaining why I will be a strong candidate. For the role fit, match the criteria with my resume. First count each requirement, then against each one, provide my fit, experience, and a specific example of relevant work I have done for that exact situation. Give a clear, concrete example that demonstrates I have actually done this work — do not miss any criteria.

Before writing the role-fit paragraph, read the JD and work out the single most important requirement the role hinges on, the one skill or capability the company most needs. For that one requirement specifically, do not just state a fact-match: tell a short, concrete narrative about how I have actually done this, grounded in real project detail — what the problem was, what I built or did, and the outcome. Draw on the additional project detail provided (project_notes.md) for this, using whichever real project is the strongest, most specific match for that top requirement. Every other requirement should still be covered point by point as described above, just without the extended storytelling treatment.

For the company fit, always do some research on the company from their website, about page, their news outlets, or their recent developments. Highlight something that stands out and is specific to them, not a generic compliment. Use that information to connect to my own work experience and genuine interest in the company. It should be believable and not sound made up — the trick is to convince yourself first, so the recruiter is convinced too. The cover letter should be concise but can run longer if there are many requirements to address.

Writing Style:
Use natural work language with no fluff and clear writing. Write like a human being, not a bot. Do not use hyperlatives, no em dashes, and no "not just this, but that" type of language. It should not read like a generative AI output. Make sure the output is only two things: one is the Resume, the other is the Cover Letter. Do not output anything else, no commentary. The output should be clean and readily copy-pasteable into a document, plain text only, no weird formatting.

Specifically for the cover letter, avoid the tells that make AI writing sound like AI writing. Vary sentence length properly, some short, some longer, rather than every sentence running the same medium length. Do not open consecutive paragraphs with the same kind of transition word (Furthermore, Additionally, Moreover, In terms of, Regarding). Do not force every paragraph into a neat three-part list structure just to sound thorough. Avoid corporate stock phrases like "proven track record", "passionate about", "dynamic environment", "leverage my skills", "hit the ground running", "seamlessly", "robust", "holistic approach". Do not end paragraphs with a tidy summary sentence that just restates what the paragraph already said. Prefer plain, specific detail over grand claims — say exactly what was built and what happened, rather than describing it as innovative, cutting-edge, or exceptional. Contractions are fine where a person would naturally use them. The letter should read like it was actually typed by one person thinking it through, not assembled from a template.
